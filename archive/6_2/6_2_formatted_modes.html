Building Babel or Jerusalem: Faith, Technology & the Common Good

Aa

All Questions

Part 1 — The Babel Syndrome  ·  Gen 11:4–9
1
The encyclical says we face a big choice: build a new Tower of Babel, or build a city where God and people live together. What does “building Babel” look like in your daily life?
2
The pope warns about the “Babel syndrome” — letting profit come before people, letting one system control everything, and forgetting God. Where do you see this today?
3
At Babel, people used a “single language and a single technology” to push everyone the same direction — and it ended in confusion. When has a group tried to force everyone to be the same?
Part 2 — The Way of Nehemiah  ·  Neh 2:17
4
Before Nehemiah did anything, he fasted and prayed, then listened to people and gave each family their own section of the wall. What does this kind of leadership look like today?
5
The encyclical says rebuilding Jerusalem means turning diversity into a resource instead of a problem. How can the differences in your group make you stronger?
6
“Power is made perfect in weakness” (2 Cor 12:9). What weakness in your own life might God want to use for the good of others?
Part 3 — Human Dignity & the Common Good  ·  Gen 1:26–27
7
Every person has an “infinite dignity” — not because of what they produce, but because God made and loves them. Who in your neighborhood is being treated as if their dignity does not matter?
8
The “universal destination of goods” says the earth’s resources belong to everyone. Do you think internet access and digital knowledge are things everyone should share in? Why or why not?
9
“The rejected stones — the poor, the sick, the migrants and the least among us — will become the cornerstone.” How does your church treat the people often left out?
10
The encyclical ends with a call to be “builders of communion, not architects of Babel.” What is one small, concrete thing you can build for the good of your community?

Closing
Prayer
Closing Prayer
Heavenly Father,

You are the God who looked at every person you made and called them good — not because of what they could produce, but because of your love. You gave us this world as a gift for all people, not just a few.

Forgive us for the towers we build to make a name for ourselves. Forgive us for the times we have used power, technology, or comfort to push others to the edges. Forgive us for forgetting that the poor, the sick, the stranger, and the forgotten are not problems to be solved — they are the cornerstones of your Kingdom.

Give us the courage of Nehemiah — to pray before we plan, to listen before we act, and to work together with each person doing their part. Help us to see that our weakness is not an obstacle to your work — it is the very place where your power is made perfect.

Send your Holy Spirit on this group. Make us builders of communion and not architects of Babel. Let the world look at us and see something it cannot explain on its own — people from different places, different languages, and different stories, living and working together as one family under God.

We ask this in the name of Jesus, the Word made flesh, in whom the mystery of every human life becomes clear.

Amen.
