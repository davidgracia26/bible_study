Unity, Diversity & the Church

From the confusion of Babel to the miracle of Pentecost — and what it means to be the Church in Houston today.

Discussion Questions

Part 1 The Tower of Babel — Genesis 11:4, 9
1
The people at Babel wanted to "make a name for ourselves." What drives that impulse — pride, fear, insecurity? Where do you see that same impulse at work in culture, institutions, or your own life?
Gen 11:4Brueggemann
2
Walter Brueggemann reads Babel as an "empire story" — humanity organizing itself around power and self-sufficiency rather than dependence on God. What empires (large or small) are you tempted to build in your own life?
Gen 11:4Brueggemann
3
God's response at Babel is to scatter. Is the scattering a punishment, a mercy, or something else entirely? What does it tell us about what God values?
Gen 11:9
4
Babel's unity was built on sameness and self-promotion. When have you seen a community — a church, a workplace, a family — try to maintain unity by suppressing difference? What was the cost?
Gen 11:4–9Hauerwas
5
Brueggemann speaks of the "prophetic imagination" — the ability to envision a world ordered differently than the empire orders it. What would it look like to imagine your community through that prophetic lens this week?
Gen 11:9Brueggemann
Part 2 The Day of Pentecost — Acts 2:4, 6
6
At Babel, one language became many. At Pentecost, many languages remain — yet each person hears. Why does the Spirit not simply restore one language? What does it mean that God speaks into diversity rather than erasing it?
Acts 2:4Acts 2:6N.T. Wright
7
N.T. Wright describes Pentecost as the launch of the "new creation" — God's project of renewal breaking into the old world. How does that frame change how you think about what the Church is supposed to be doing?
Acts 2:4N.T. Wright
8
"Each one was hearing them speak in his own language" (Acts 2:6). The gospel arrived in people's mother tongues. What does that say about how God values particularity — your background, language, and story — rather than demanding you conform to someone else's?
Acts 2:6
9
If Babel is about humanity grasping for heaven on its own terms, and Pentecost is about heaven descending on God's terms — what are the "Babel projects" the Church is sometimes tempted to substitute for genuine Pentecost community?
Acts 2:4Hauerwas
10
The crowd at Pentecost was "bewildered" (Acts 2:6) — the word implies being out of one's mind. When have you experienced the Church doing something genuinely surprising or disorienting in the best possible way?
Acts 2:6
Part 3 The Church as Social Miracle — Ephesians 2:14 & Houston
11
Paul says Christ has broken down "the dividing wall of hostility" (Eph 2:14). What walls — racial, economic, generational, political — are hardest for your church to let Christ break down? Why?
Eph 2:14N.T. Wright
12
Stanley Hauerwas argues the Church is a distinct political community — not a chaplain to the existing culture, but a "contrast society" that shows the world a different way to be human together. Does your church feel like a contrast society? Should it?
Eph 2:14Hauerwas
13
Richard Beck says, "The Church is called to be a social miracle." A miracle is something that cannot be explained by natural causes alone. Is your experience of the Church miraculous — or explainable? What would change if it were truly a miracle?
Richard BeckEph 2:14
14
Houston is one of the most ethnically diverse cities on earth — more than 145 languages spoken. Given Acts 2 and Ephesians 2:14, what is God uniquely calling the Church in Houston to demonstrate to the watching world?
Acts 2:6Eph 2:14Houston
15
The arc of Scripture runs from Babel's scattering to Pentecost's gathering to Revelation's vision of "every tribe, tongue, and nation" around the throne (Rev 7:9). How does that end-of-the-story vision change how you live and worship right now — this week, in this city?
Gen 11Acts 2Eph 2:14Rev 7:9

Voices for the Discussion

The church does not have a social ethic; the church is a social ethic.
Stanley Hauerwas
Theologian & ethicist; Duke Divinity School
The church is called to be a sign, instrument, and foretaste of the new creation that God is bringing about through Jesus Christ. It is not a club for the like-minded.

Closing Prayer

▶

Heavenly Father,

You are the God who scattered the proud at Babel and gathered the humble at Pentecost. You are the God who tears down walls that we insist on building and builds bridges we are afraid to cross.

Forgive us for the towers we construct to make a name for ourselves — the ways we cling to sameness, protect our comfort, and call it community. Forgive us for the walls we have allowed to stand — in our hearts, in our church, and in our city.

Pour out your Spirit on us again. Teach us to hear one another across every difference of language, background, and experience — not because diversity is easy, but because you are worth it. Let us be, in Houston and in our neighborhood, a sign that the dividing wall has truly been broken down.

Make us a social miracle. Not because we are talented or organized or well-intentioned, but because your Son is our peace and your Spirit is our power. May the watching world look at us and find no natural explanation.

We ask this in the name of Jesus, who is himself the peace between all things.

Amen.
