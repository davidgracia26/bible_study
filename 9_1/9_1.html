Ordinary Glory  ·  Bible Study — September 1

Love Like Dolly Parton: Give, Welcome, Transform

Aa

All Questions

Part 1 — God’s Story  ·  John 3:17
1
John 3:17 says God did not send Jesus to condemn the world, but to save it. Many of us grew up thinking God is always watching to catch our mistakes. How does knowing God’s purpose is to save you, not condemn you, change the way you see him? How does it change the way you look at other people?
2
Dolly Parton says, “God is my co-writer and my co-pilot.” A co-writer helps write the story, and a co-pilot helps fly the plane — they do not take over completely, but you do not do it alone either. Do you let God co-write your story and co-pilot your life? What is one decision or problem you are facing right now that you can bring to him this week?
Part 2 — Give People Language for Their Lives  ·  Psalm 13 · Luke 18 · Romans 8
3
Sarah Smarsh says country music gave women a “language” to talk about feelings that were hard to say out loud. In Psalm 13, David is honest with God about his sadness and fear — he does not hide his feelings. Why is it hard for us to say our true feelings, even to God? What words could you use this week to tell God how you really feel?
4
The blind man in Luke 18 would not stay quiet — he kept shouting until Jesus heard him, even when the crowd told him to be quiet. Romans 8 says all of creation is groaning, and we wait for God with hope, even when we cannot yet see the answer. What are you “crying out” to God about right now? How can you keep asking and wait for God’s answer with hope, instead of giving up or staying quiet?
Part 3 — Make “Those People” Your People  ·  Luke 15:1–2
5
Jesus ate with tax collectors and sinners — the people others called “those people.” The religious leaders muttered and did not like it. Who are the people that others in your life, church, or town call “those people”? What would it look like for you to welcome them the way Jesus did?
6
Dolly Parton says, “I just love people. I do not judge or criticize or condemn.” That is a hard way to live! Think of someone you have judged, criticized, or written off recently. What would it look like to love that person the way Dolly describes, instead of judging them?
Part 4 — Transform Inherited Pain  ·  1 Peter 4 · 2 Cor 1 · John 20
7
God gives each of us a gift to help other people (1 Peter 4:10). When we go through hard times, God comforts us. Then we can use that same comfort to help someone else who is hurting (2 Corinthians 1:3–4). This means even our pain can become a gift for another person. Think of one hard thing you went through. How could God use it to help or comfort someone else?
8
Thomas doubted until he saw Jesus’ wounds, and Jesus still called him blessed for later believing. Dolly Parton says, “You don’t even have to believe in anything to be good to people. You should just know you should be a good human being.” Paul says the greatest of faith, hope, and love is love. Do you agree that love matters even more than being sure about everything? How can you show love this week to someone, even while you or they still have doubts?

Prayer
Closing Prayer
Dear God,

Thank you that you did not send Jesus to judge us, but to save us. Help us to let you write our story, instead of trying to write it alone.

Give us the words to tell you our true feelings, even when it is hard, like David did when he was sad and afraid.

Help us to cry out to you like the blind man in the road, and to wait for your answer with hope, even when we have to wait a long time.

Teach us to love “those people” — the ones others push away — the same way Jesus welcomed sinners and ate with them. Help us not to judge, but to love.

And Father, take the hard and painful things from our past, and turn them into comfort and love for someone else. Like Thomas, help us to believe, even when we cannot see. And above everything — faith, hope, and love — help us to hold on to love, because it is the greatest of all.

We ask this in the name of Jesus, who loved us first. Amen.
